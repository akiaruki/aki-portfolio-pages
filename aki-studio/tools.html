The tools / A closer look

A little understanding.
More room to create.

Twelve familiar tools, with space for your own way of seeing. Learn what each one changes, then follow your eye.

Every tool is free. No subscriptions or in-app purchases.

01 / 12

Crop

Choose what stays.

Like choosing the edges of a print, cropping decides what belongs in the frame. Straightening helps the photograph sit the way you intended.

In Aki Studio
Freeform, original and preset ratios; custom and saved ratios; portrait/landscape; a straightening ruler, 90° rotation and horizontal mirror. Drag or pinch to position and zoom the photograph, then apply the crop.
Useful when
Trim a distraction, level a horizon or prepare a photograph for a particular shape.
02 / 12

Develop

Set the foundation.

The familiar light and color adjustments you reach for first. Start with the overall balance, then bring attention to the parts of the photograph that matter.

In Aki Studio
Exposure, contrast, highlights and shadows; warmth, tint, vibrance and saturation; texture, clarity and dehaze. Fine tuning adds whites, blacks, faded blacks, highlight roll-off and color protection. Auto offers a starting adjustment, with a preview histogram to help read the tones.
Useful when
Brighten a dim frame, soften strong highlights, open shadows or adjust the warmth before adding a Look.
03 / 12

Curves

Shape light with a line.

A curve maps existing tones to new ones. Lift part of the line to brighten those tones, or lower it to darken them. Separate color channels let you shift their balance too.

In Aki Studio
RGB, Red, Green and Blue curves with movable points, point selection, add/remove and reset controls. Input and Output are readouts. Tone offers Highlights, Lights, Darks and Shadows sliders with adjustable boundaries.
Useful when
Build a gentle S-curve for contrast, lift darker tones for a softer finish or refine a color cast.
04 / 12

Color Mixer

Give each color a direction.

Hue changes a color’s character, saturation changes its intensity, and luminance changes its brightness. Work on a color range while keeping the rest of your palette in view.

In Aki Studio
Choose Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Aqua, Blue, Purple or Magenta, then adjust Hue, Saturation and Luminance. Reset the selected color or the whole mixer.
Useful when
Quiet a strong green, soften a bright blue or bring a flower’s color closer to the feeling you remember.
05 / 12

Detail

Balance crispness and softness.

Sharpening emphasizes edges. Noise reduction smooths visible noise. Finding a balance helps preserve the texture you want to see.

In Aki Studio
Sharpening and Noise reduction, with a tool reset.
Useful when
Give small details a little definition or reduce distracting noise in a low-light photograph. Compare as you go to keep the texture natural.
06 / 12

B&W Mixer

Let color become tone.

A black-and-white photograph still begins with color. The mixer lets those original colors become lighter or darker shades of gray.

In Aki Studio
Enable Black & white, then adjust the brightness of Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Aqua, Blue, Purple and Magenta.
Useful when
Separate a blue sky from clouds, bring a subject forward or give foliage more depth in monochrome.
07 / 12

Split Toning

Two tones. One atmosphere.

Add different colors to darker and brighter areas. The balance between them can make the photograph feel warmer, cooler or more expressive.

In Aki Studio
Shadow hue and saturation, Highlight hue and saturation, and Balance.
Useful when
Pair cool shadows with warm highlights, or add a restrained tint to a black-and-white edit.
08 / 12

Optics

Refine the lens-like edges.

Manual optical adjustments change the way lines bend and light falls toward the edges of the photograph.

In Aki Studio
Distortion and Lens shading.
Useful when
Adjust visible bowing in lines or compensate for darker or lighter edges. Tune these controls by eye for the photograph in front of you.
09 / 12

Geometry

Find a steadier perspective.

Tilting a camera changes the apparent angle of a subject. Perspective controls help reshape that relationship after the photograph is taken.

In Aki Studio
Vertical perspective and Horizontal perspective.
Useful when
Refine converging building lines or a subject photographed from one side. Check the composition again with Crop after adjusting.
10 / 12

Effects

Finish with character.

The finishing touches that change atmosphere: light around the edges, a glow in brighter areas and a little grain across the image.

In Aki Studio
Vignette, Highlight glow and Grain. Fine tuning adds Vignette softness, Grain size and Highlight softness.
Useful when
Draw attention toward the center, soften bright light or add texture. Try small changes first, then compare with your starting point.
11 / 12

Frame

Give the photograph room.

Like a border around a print, a frame separates the picture from its surroundings and gives the composition a little breathing space.

In Aki Studio
Black or white frame color and an Instant style. Drag the frame edges or pinch to adjust the border.
Useful when
Create a clean presentation or an instant-print feeling before saving and sharing.
12 / 12

Date

Keep a moment in view.

A visible date stamp adds a small piece of context to the photograph, with the familiar feeling of a dated print.

In Aki Studio
Choose a date and one of four corners. Use automatic, white, black or custom color, or remove the date.
Useful when
Mark a trip, a celebration or an everyday memory. Choose the date you want the finished photograph to show.

Make a start

One adjustment at a time.

Begin with Crop and Develop, explore a Look, then refine the details. Compare your edit with the original and use Undo or Redo as you experiment. Your tools can be reordered to suit your own process.
